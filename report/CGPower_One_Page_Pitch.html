CG Power and Industrial Solutions (NSE: CGPOWER)

SELL
TP ₹402 · price ₹879 · −54%

A strong business priced for a decade it has never delivered: ₹879 needs ~35% revenue growth a year for ten years at a 12% free-cash-flow margin.

Three reasons

  1. The price implies an extreme path. A reverse DCF at a 14.8% WACC puts FY36 revenue at ~₹2.5 lakh Cr, 20x FY26 and 5.7x the combined FY26 revenue of ABB India, Siemens, Hitachi Energy India and GE Vernova T&D India. It also needs a 12% FCF margin every year, matching CG's single best year since FY22. At an 11% WACC the price still needs 25% growth for a decade.
  2. Cash is not keeping up with profit. FY26 receivables +46% vs revenue +25%; operating cash flow was 43% of EBITDA; free cash flow −₹72 Cr as capex reached 6% of revenue. The semiconductor business cost ₹111 Cr in FY26 and ₹43 Cr in Q1 FY27 alone. Q1 growth slowed to 14% (Industrial Systems +6%), with EBITDA margin at 12.1%.
  3. Expensive even against expensive peers. CG trades at 115x FY26 P/E and 82x EV/EBITDA against peer medians of 86x and 65x. At peer multiples it is worth ₹682 (−22%; −17% on trailing twelve months to June 2026); our DCF gives ₹122 (−86%). Either method alone is a SELL.

What the bulls are right about

  • Backlog ₹18,965 Cr (+45%); Power Systems backlog 2.8x segment revenue.
  • Power Systems revenue +46% in FY26, +31% in Q1 FY27, at a 24% EBITDA margin.
  • ₹900 Cr US data-centre transformer order; exports only ~5% of revenue.
  • OSAT plant backed by ₹4,901 Cr of government capital support.

All of this is real, and most of the Street agrees. The question is whether it is worth 35% compounding for a decade; even after cuts, my forecast assumes more than Q1 FY27 delivered.

What would change my mind

  • Growth above 25% with FCF margin moving towards double digits.
  • A profitable semiconductor ramp at G2 (due end-2026).
  • Receivables growing in line with revenue again.

Risks to the call

  • Sector multiples stay at 65x+ EV/EBITDA for years; the Street is mostly bullish.
  • Faster export and data-centre order conversion.
  • Lower rates: the 10-year yield is at a two-year high.
The commodity angle: raw materials are 69% of revenue. IEEMA's price-variation formulas, the industry's own cost weights for transformers and motors, put copper at ~27% and electrical steel at ~24% of CG's material cost once weighted by business mix. A 10% copper rise cuts EBITDA margin ~94 bps (7% of FY27E EBITDA) at 50% pass-through. Commodity costs were one reason Industrial Systems' margin fell from 12.8% to 10.6% in FY26 and 9.6% in Q1 FY27.
